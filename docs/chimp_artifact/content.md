The last story ended with the walls holding. A researcher asked a question in
plain English, a Guide turned it into a strict little form, a raven carried
only that form across a thin link, and the keep checked every answer before a
single rounded number came back out. Reassuring — and limited. All the actual
*thinking* still happened outside, one question at a time, by hand.

This is the more ambitious sequel, and the more nervous one: we let a capable
analyst work **inside** the keep. It can ask many questions, run real
statistics, follow the evidence where it leads. The obvious fear is just as
obvious as before. So, same trick as the bestiary: turn the machinery into a
character I can actually hold in my head — and keep the real record one click
behind it. Its name is **Chimp**, after the deliberately capability-limited
ship's mind in Peter Watts' *The Freeze-Frame Revolution*: clever enough to run
the mission, bounded so it can never outwit the crew.

## The Analyst Inside

![The Analyst Inside](chimp/slide_01_the_analyst_inside.png)

Here is the whole cast in one frame. The **Researcher** still stands outside;
the **Keep** still holds the data behind its padlocked gate. What is new is the
lit window: an analyst now lives *within* the walls. The promise is that this
changes what the AI can *do* for you without changing what can *leave*. The
motto for the whole deck is the one to hold onto — **only vetted summaries
cross the wall** — because everything below is just the machinery that makes
that true whether Chimp is dim, brilliant, or actively misbehaving.

## Chimp in the Enclosure

![Chimp in the Enclosure](chimp/slide_02_chimp_in_the_enclosure.png)

Chimp is plainly capable and plainly *contained* — a caged genius, happy at its
desk. It sits behind glass, next to the sealed diaries it may never open. Its
only tools are a small fixed rack of instruments: an abacus, a chart, a clock.
It cannot write code. It cannot write a database query. It cannot ask for one
person's record. Its scribbled working notes drift toward the glass and stop
dead — they are its own, and they never leave the room. The banner reads
**clever, and kept**, and that is the reassuring half of the story: the AI in
the vault is a modest one, on a short menu, by deliberate design.

## A Whole Question, One Gate

![A Whole Question, One Gate](chimp/slide_03_a_whole_question_one_gate.png)

This is where Chimp earns its keep. The Researcher no longer sends one little
form; they send a whole *question* — *is late-night phone use linked to
gambling?* Chimp works through it inside: a mean here, a correlation there, a
model adjusted for a confounder, a check for a false trail. It stitches the
findings into a single **dossier** and seals it. Only that sealed dossier
crosses back through the gate, to a Scribe who writes the plain-English answer.
Notice the slips stamped **refused**: when the keep says a particular analysis
would expose too few people, Chimp gets the same refusal a human would, keeps
that slip inside, and says so in its report. Every one of its questions ran the
identical gauntlet the old single query did.

## The Menu on the Wall

![The Menu on the Wall](chimp/slide_04_the_menu_on_the_wall.png)

This is why the modesty is not just a promise. Chimp's *entire* vocabulary is a
short menu of fill-in-the-blank sentences — the average of ▢ by ▢, the
correlation of ▢ and ▢, a regression, a time series — filled in from a fixed,
allowlisted catalogue and nothing else. The whole set is finite. You can print
it on one page and read every sentence Chimp is capable of forming; a machine
already checks all of them, including the ones nobody will ever type. The three
crossed-out tags matter as much as the menu: *write code*, *read one person's
record*, *invent a new question* — none of these is a sentence Chimp can even
say. **A fixed menu. No sixth kind of sentence.**

## The Sealed Plan

![The Sealed Plan](chimp/slide_05_the_sealed_plan.png)

There is one genuinely clever thing a human analyst does that we let Chimp do
too — and it is the one that needed a leash. When a model is refused because a
group is too small, a person shrugs and drops that group. But *which* group was
too small is itself a secret. So Chimp may only do it under a rule: it writes
its plan on a scroll and presses a **wax seal into the tamper-proof logbook
before it looks**; a plain clockwork machine — not the AI — runs the sealed
plan; and that single peek at what the keep withheld is **paid for from a tiny
jar of tokens**. Spend the jar and the peek is refused. So Chimp can be
genuinely useful without ever making a free, unrecorded choice about the hidden
data. When a proper privacy budget arrives, this jar is exactly what it becomes.

## The Window

![The Window](chimp/slide_06_the_window.png)

One last reassurance, about control. Whether an analyst lives inside the keep at
all is the **keeper's** decision, set at the gate, turned to `ON` or `OFF` by
the Castellan with the brass keys — never something a visitor can flip from
outside. The Researcher only gets to *speak through a window*: a question goes
in, a sealed dossier comes back, and Chimp, its notes and the diaries all stay
within. That is the whole architecture in one picture. **The operator decides;
the visitor only asks; and the wall, as ever, is what actually keeps the
promise.**
